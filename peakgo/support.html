For iPhone on iOS 17 or later

Why is altitude off, or why does it show "--"?

Altitude has two sources: GPS and the barometer. GPS vertical accuracy is much weaker than horizontal, and barometric altitude depends on a sea-level pressure baseline. The app fuses both and always shows the uncertainty range and source (System Fusion / Calibrated / GPS Coarse) next to the number.

When signal is poor or no source can give a trustworthy value, it shows "--" rather than inventing a number. That is deliberate: a falsely precise altitude is more dangerous than none.

How to do Known-Point Calibration

  1. Stand at a place with a known altitude (official benchmark, map contour, airport elevation).
  2. Open the pressure detail page and choose Known-Point Calibration, then enter the true altitude.
  3. The app derives sea-level pressure and stores the baseline. The source label becomes "Calibrated".

Calibration is not permanent: weather systems make pressure drift, so recalibrate every few days or when travelling between regions.

Devices without a barometer

Some earlier iPhone models have no barometer. In that case the pressure page states that no trend can be recorded, while altitude still comes from GPS (at lower accuracy). Level and compass are unaffected.

What if I denied a permission?

  • Location: iOS Settings → PeakGo → Location → While Using the App. Used for altitude, coordinates and true north.
  • Camera / Photos: used by the watermark camera. Photos are only written to your library; the app never reads it.
  • Motion & Fitness: used for barometer and altimeter data, plus compass tilt compensation.

Denying any of them leaves the rest of the app usable; the app never strands you on an empty screen.

Subscription: cancel, restore, free first year

  • Cancel: iOS Settings → Apple ID → Subscriptions → PeakGo → Cancel Subscription. Deleting the app does not cancel it.
  • Restore: tap "Restore Purchases" on the subscription page after reinstalling or switching devices.
  • Refunds: handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.
  • Why no "first year free"? A free trial can be used only once per Apple ID per subscription group. If your account used it before, the page shows the yearly price instead. That is Apple's rule and the app cannot bypass it.

Note: altitude, barometer, compass and level are free permanently and never stop working without a subscription. The subscription is voluntary support that funds future instruments and themes.

I changed the language but the UI didn't change

After choosing a language in Settings, reopen the app for it to take effect (the app does not force-restart itself). Changes to the system language apply on the next launch too.

Why is there no iPad version?

The first release is iPhone-only. Landscape and iPad multi-window layouts have not been verified screen by screen, and shipping untested layouts is worse than not claiming support. iPad users can still install the iPhone-compatible version.
